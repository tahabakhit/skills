# User research

Separate planning a real study from analyzing existing material. Neither produces observations merely because a model can write a plausible participant response.

## Plan a study

Name the decision the study should inform. Compare candidate methods against that decision, available participants, accessibility needs, time, and risk. Interviews explore accounts and motivations. Task-based usability work observes whether people can complete a specified task. Surveys estimate responses within the sampling design. A method recommendation can be useful when requested, but the method's cost and inference limits must be visible.

Draft recruitment criteria, a screener, a neutral discussion/task guide, and a data-handling plan only as needed. Describe the study's purpose truthfully. Do not conceal research as a casual conversation or disguised selling. Obtain appropriate permission and consent for contacting participants, recording, storing, or sharing their material. Drafting an invitation is not sending it.

Recruit to cover the decision, not only the happiest or most profitable customers. Include excluded groups and nonresponse risks in the plan. A small number of conversations can reveal problems. It does not automatically establish saturation, population prevalence, or a complete persona set.

## Analyze real material

Retain source and participant identifiers separately from direct identifying details. Keep originals where policy permits. Flag uncertain speaker labels or transcription rather than silently changing meaning.

Apply a stated extraction/coding rule consistently. Retain contrary cases. Count distinct participants separately from mentions, repeated tickets, and duplicate transcripts. Link consequential themes to exact quotations and source locations. A quotation that occurs several times from one participant is not several independent respondents.

Separate what was said, what was observed, and the analyst's interpretation. Do not infer emotion or intent merely from wording when alternatives remain plausible. Offer competing interpretations and testable hypotheses where useful. Avoid categorical statements that participants want something unless the evidence supports that wording.

A convenience sample's mention count is not a percentage of all customers. Missing responses are unknown. Explain recruitment, sample composition, question framing, coding choices, and material gaps. Synthetic examples may illustrate a plan only when clearly labeled. Never mix them into real findings.

## Return useful work

Match the deliverable to the request: study plan, guide, traceable themes, quote bank, issue map, or research report. Recommendations may be conditional on the evidence. Preserve the human's decision authority without refusing every request for analysis or method advice. Name the next observation that would discriminate between competing explanations.
