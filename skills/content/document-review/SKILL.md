# Document review

Establish the requested review scope and use the evidence provided. Check authorization before opening confidential material. A local shell does not make a hosted model local. Do not send material to another service or publish comments merely because review was requested.

Map the main claims to their support. Distinguish direct observations, calculations, estimates, opinions, recommendations, and causal assertions. Recalculate consequential arithmetic. Check units, time periods, sample definitions, and denominators before comparing numbers. Invited people, respondents, observations, and the wider population are different denominators. Missing responses are unknown, not negative responses.

A valid calculation can still support an invalid comparison. Compare recruitment, population, task, question wording, timing, and design before treating two samples as a trend. Do not infer causality from an uncontrolled comparison or turn a prototype result into production evidence. Preserve legitimate descriptive findings even when stronger interpretations fail.

Prioritize errors that could change a decision. Give each major finding a location or quoted claim, the reason it fails, its consequence, and the least burdensome adequate correction. A narrower claim may be sufficient. Request new evidence only where the requested conclusion needs it. Do not invent citations, statistical tests, missing results, or certainty.

When asked for a corrected summary, retain supported facts and state the important limitations directly. Preserve exact quotations unless the user authorizes changing them. Keep review and rewriting separate when only review was requested. Follow the requested format and length, then stop at the review endpoint. A fluent summary and a complete bibliography do not establish that the underlying conclusions are true.
