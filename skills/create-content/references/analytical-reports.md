# Analytical reports

Define each quantity before calculating or comparing it. Record the population, unit, period, geography, measure, denominator, currency and price basis where relevant. Label source facts, analyst calculations, assumptions, scenarios, and recommendations separately.

A valid formula does not ensure compatible inputs. Check whether components overlap, whether one is a resale of another, and whether a stock is being added to a flow. A source's total may cover a broader category than the requested market. Preserve the unexplained remainder instead of forcing a match.

For important calculations, retain the formula, source inputs, transformations, intermediate values, rounding, and output units. Check arithmetic independently of the prose. For repeated or large calculations, use an approved available engine and retain enough information to reproduce the result. Do not claim reproducibility merely because a script file exists.

Compare sources by definitions, observation period, methods, original producer, and revision vintage. Repeated publication of one estimate does not create independent confirmation. Explain unresolved differences and how they affect the recommendation.

Use scenarios to make assumptions explicit. Do not guarantee market share or call a scenario band a confidence interval. Report sensitivity to assumptions that could change a decision. An apparently precise forecast needs evidence and an appropriate model, not more decimal places.

When combining surveys or studies over time, compare population, recruitment, instrument wording, task, mode, and analysis choices. A sample or instrument change can create a false trend. Preserve descriptive results while narrowing unsupported causal or population claims.

Lead with findings and decision implications. Include uncertainty, contrary evidence, and the smallest adequate next proof. Charts are optional. When used, make units, denominators, source identities, observed versus forecast periods, and material uncertainty visible. Never create data to fill a preferred chart or report length.

Structured ledgers and validators help detect missing fields or declared inconsistencies. They do not prove a source supports a proposition, that coverage is truly disjoint, or that a model is scientifically appropriate.
